Choosing loss functions for imbalanced segmentation masks

notes · deep learning · semantic segmentation

Segmentation tasks like mapping glaciers, roads, or lesions from imagery share an awkward property: the class you care about most is usually the smallest one in the image. A glacier mask might be 5% foreground pixels against a 95% background of rock and sky. Trained naively with pixel-wise cross-entropy, a model can hit 95% accuracy by predicting background everywhere, and still be completely useless.

Why cross-entropy alone fails here

Cross-entropy treats every pixel as an independent classification decision, weighted equally. With a heavy class imbalance, gradient contributions from the minority class get drowned out early in training, and the model settles into a "predict the majority class" local minimum that's very hard to escape without help.

Dice loss: optimizing overlap directly

Dice loss instead optimizes the overlap between predicted and ground-truth masks directly, it's derived from the Dice coefficient, 2·|A∩B| / (|A|+|B|). Because it's a ratio rather than a per-pixel sum, it's naturally less sensitive to class imbalance: a model gets credit for correctly identifying the small foreground region regardless of how much background surrounds it.

In practice, Dice loss on its own can be unstable early in training (empty or near-empty prediction masks produce ill-behaved gradients), so it's common to combine it with cross-entropy as a weighted sum, cross-entropy to stabilize early training, Dice to push overlap quality later on.

Tversky loss: tuning the precision/recall trade-off

Tversky loss generalizes Dice with two extra parameters, α and β, that separately weight false positives and false negatives. For a problem like glacier mapping, where under-predicting glacier extent might be a worse error than over-predicting it (or vice versa, depending on the downstream use), Tversky lets you bias training toward the error you'd rather tolerate. Setting α = β = 0.5 recovers plain Dice loss.

Boundary-aware losses

Region-based losses like Dice and Tversky optimize overlap but are fairly indifferent to how clean the boundary is, a mask that's shifted a few pixels inward can still score well. For tasks where the boundary itself matters (a glacier's terminus position, a road's edge), boundary or contour losses that penalize distance from the ground-truth boundary directly can meaningfully sharpen results, usually added as a secondary term alongside a region-based loss rather than used alone.

A reasonable starting recipe

  • Start with a weighted combination: loss = CE + Dice, equally weighted.
  • If false negatives on the minority class are costly, move to Tversky with β > α.
  • If boundary precision matters more than raw overlap, add a boundary term with a small weight (it should refine, not dominate, the loss).
  • Always check per-class metrics (IoU per class, not just mean IoU). A good mean can hide a bad minority class.
