What I'm working on

Hi, I'm Mukesh Dewangan.

I'm a Bachelor's student at IIT Gandhinagar, working on machine learning and computer vision. My work spans document intelligence, visual forensics, and computer vision systems built for real-world, imperfect data - not clean benchmarks.

I’m interested in two main questions:

  • How do we build models that survive real, messy data?
  • How do we combine a few imperfect signals into a decision we'd actually trust?

Where I've worked

Machine Learning Intern
May to Jul 2026
Bank of Baroda, Mumbai

Built a fraud detection system for PAN cards by testing seven different approaches - from OCR rule checks to vision transformers to anomaly detection - and combining the four that actually worked into one pipeline, shipped with a dashboard for bank staff to use directly.

The more useful result was figuring out why the rest didn't work: found a silent bug that had frozen a model's training for weeks, and proved that the hardest forgery type - a single digit swapped in a re-saved JPEG - is undetectable by any image-based method, full stop. That's the kind of finding that saves the next person months.

Machine Learning Research Intern
May to Aug 2025
IIT Roorkee

Mapped Himalayan glaciers from 18-band satellite imagery - most of which isn't even visible light - and got to 82.4% mean IoU by combining two architectures that make different mistakes instead of betting on one.

Glaciers are a small, thin sliver of every image, so a model that just optimizes for overall accuracy learns to ignore them entirely. Testing three different loss functions to fix that, and running a sensitivity analysis to find out which of the 18 bands the model was actually using, mattered more than the architecture choice itself.

Things I've built

01
Glacier Mapping, Himalayan Satellite Imagery

Segmented glaciers across five Himalayan regions from 18-band satellite data — elevation, thermal, and SWIR bands, not just color. A ResUNet trained with a Focal+Dice+Tversky loss combination reached 82% mean IoU on this class-imbalanced problem, where glaciers are a tiny sliver of each image. Currently ensembling it with a SegFormer model to push performance further.

PAN Card Forgery Detection Pipeline

A four-stage document forensics system for a banking risk setting — OCR validation, tamper detection, visual forensics, and a trained classifier, combined through majority voting. Tested seven different detection approaches and found that single-character forgeries are undetectable from pixels alone once a document is re-compressed — a real limit, not a modeling gap.
